The Recap v2 · Diagnostic-First Architecture · 2026-05-04

Two zones. One artifact. The share button knows the difference.

The Recap is two artifacts in one frame. Above the rule: the diagnostic dashboard a parent opens to answer did the week hold together? — pace, hours, completion, carryforwards. For her eyes only. Hours and percentages do not survive contact with audiences trained on seat time. They never share.

Below the rule: the artifact she shares. Hero, Story, mastery shifts as concept-language accomplishments, voice line. The share gesture captures this zone only — not by the parent's discipline, but by architecture. There is no version of the share that includes the hours number, because the share button cannot reach above the rule.

The architectural rule. A 1px line in the deeper warm tone (#D8CFB8) with a 40px saffron accent at center. Quiet but visible. The parent reads it as a fold — above is hers, below is what the artifact tells the world. The eyebrow on each zone (FOR YOU · Private | THE WEEK · Shareable) reinforces what the rule means without explaining it. Two visual cues, one architectural decision.

What lives where, and why.

For You
Pace status, total hours, hours by subject, blocks completed, carryforwards by name. Diagnostic. Invites comparison to seat-time when read by audiences trained on traditional school. Cannot share.
Shareable
Hero photograph, Story prose, mastery shifts as concept-language accomplishments, voice line. Expressed in subject-matter language, not metric language. Reads correctly to any audience. Default share.
Discipline
The share button cannot reach the For You zone. Not gated, not hidden — architecturally outside the share gesture's scope. The parent never has to remember what's safe to share.
Why mastery shifts share but hours don't. A grandparent reads Jack — triangulation, halfway to full and thinks my grandson learned a real geometry concept. A grandparent reads 14 hours this week and thinks that's only three hours a day, is that enough? Same week, two readings, depending entirely on whether the artifact uses subject-matter language or metric language. The share zone uses subject-matter language by design.

What goes to grandma. The full emotional artifact: photograph, voice line, Story prose, mastery shifts as concept-language accomplishments, the closing voice signature. No hours number. No completion ratio. No carryforwards. Nothing that invites the seat-time comparison.

The mastery shifts are the diagnostic substance that survives sharing. They tell the recipient what was actually learned in subject-matter terms — triangulation, multi-digit multiplication — rather than in time terms. A reader who has never homeschooled reads these and understands the work was real. The same reader looking at 14 hours would compare it to a school day and conclude the opposite.

Architecturally: the share button captures everything between the architectural rule and the closing voice line. The For You zone above the rule is unreachable from the share gesture. The parent doesn't choose what to share — the artifact has already chosen for her.
